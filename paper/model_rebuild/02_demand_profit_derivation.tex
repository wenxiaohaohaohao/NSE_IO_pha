% Phase 2 only: demand, product pricing, operating profit, and commercial PV.
\section{Demand and Operating Profit}
\label{sec:p02-demand-profit}

This section derives the commercial value kernel used by later manufacturing
routes. It conditions on a project value shifter \(q>0\) and a positive
marginal manufacturing cost \(c>0\). The route technologies that generate
\(c\) are deliberately deferred to Phase~3. Product price \(p\) is distinct
from the qualified manufacturing-service price \(p_m\).

Let \(\mathsf Y\) denote physical product-output units per operating period.
Both \(p\) and \(c\) are measured in \(\mathsf C/\mathsf Y\), demand is
measured in \(\mathsf Y\) per period, one-period operating profit in
\(\mathsf C\) per period, and present value in \(\mathsf C\) per commercially
active product.

\subsection{Residual demand and the pricing problem}
\label{subsec:p02-demand-pricing}

Residual demand for a successfully commercialized project is
\begin{equation}
  y(p;q)=Aq p^{-\varepsilon},
  \qquad A>0,\quad q>0,\quad \varepsilon>1.
  \label{eq:p02-demand}
\end{equation}
The scale \(A\), project shifter \(q\), and elasticity \(\varepsilon\) are
exogenous to the institutional regime. Conditional on \(q\) and \(c\), the
developer chooses product price from \(p\in[c,\infty)\):
\begin{equation}
  \max_{p\geq c}\;(p-c)Aq p^{-\varepsilon}.
  \label{eq:p02-pricing-problem}
\end{equation}
Holding \(A,q,c,\varepsilon\) fixed, the derivative with respect to the
product price is
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial}{\partial p}
  \left[(p-c)Aq p^{-\varepsilon}\right]
  &=Aq p^{-\varepsilon}
    -\varepsilon Aq(p-c)p^{-\varepsilon-1} \notag\\
  &=Aq p^{-\varepsilon-1}
    \left[\varepsilon c-(\varepsilon-1)p\right].
  \label{eq:p02-price-foc}
\end{align}
Since \(Aq p^{-\varepsilon-1}>0\), the derivative has the sign of the
bracketed affine term. That term crosses zero exactly once, giving
\begin{equation}
  p^*(c)=\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon-1}c.
  \label{eq:p02-optimal-price}
\end{equation}
Because \(\varepsilon>1\), \(p^*(c)>c\), so the solution is feasible and
interior.

For an independent second-order check, differentiate
\eqref{eq:p02-price-foc}. At the stationary point, the term multiplying the
derivative of \(p^{-\varepsilon-1}\) vanishes, leaving
\begin{equation}
  \left.
  \frac{\partial^2}{\partial p^2}
  \left[(p-c)Aq p^{-\varepsilon}\right]
  \right|_{p=p^*(c)}
  =-Aq(\varepsilon-1)
   \left[p^*(c)\right]^{-\varepsilon-1}<0.
  \label{eq:p02-price-soc}
\end{equation}
The solution is also the unique global maximum. At the lower boundary
\(p=c\), profit is zero and the derivative is positive. The derivative is
positive below \(p^*(c)\) and negative above it, while profit converges to zero
as \(p\) tends to infinity. Hence there is no competing boundary maximum.

\subsection{Optimized one-period operating profit}
\label{subsec:p02-operating-profit}

Substituting \eqref{eq:p02-optimal-price} into the objective gives
\begin{align}
  \pi(q,c)
  &=\left(\frac{c}{\varepsilon-1}\right)
    Aq\left(\frac{\varepsilon c}{\varepsilon-1}\right)^{-\varepsilon}
    \notag\\
  &=Aq
    \frac{(\varepsilon-1)^{\varepsilon-1}}
         {\varepsilon^{\varepsilon}}
    c^{1-\varepsilon}.
  \label{eq:p02-operating-profit}
\end{align}
The units are consistent: the price--cost margin is in
\(\mathsf C/\mathsf Y\), demand is in \(\mathsf Y\) per period, and their
product is in \(\mathsf C\) per period.

Holding \(A,\varepsilon\), and the non-differentiated argument fixed,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\pi(q,c)}{\partial q}
  =\frac{\pi(q,c)}{q}>0,
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial\pi(q,c)}{\partial c}
  =(1-\varepsilon)\frac{\pi(q,c)}{c}<0.
  \label{eq:p02-profit-derivatives}
\end{equation}
The cost elasticity of optimized operating profit is \(1-\varepsilon\).
These are continuous conditional derivatives in \(q\) and \(c\), not a
derivative with respect to the binary institutional regime.

\subsection{Present value conditional on commercialization}
\label{subsec:p02-present-value}

Let \(\beta\in(0,1)\) be the one-period discount factor. Conditional on an
already commercialized product, let \(\varphi\in[0,1)\) be the probability
that it remains commercially active for one more period. The parameter
\(\varphi\) is not the downstream realization probability \(s(q)\): the
latter applies before the operating stream begins and is introduced into
route values only in a later phase.

With a stationary conditional operating profit, the expected present value is
the convergent geometric sum
\begin{align}
  R(q,c)
  &=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(\beta\varphi)^t\pi(q,c) \notag\\
  &=\frac{\pi(q,c)}{1-\beta\varphi},
  \qquad 0\leq\beta\varphi<1.
  \label{eq:p02-present-value}
\end{align}
Consequently, holding \(\beta,\varphi,A,\varepsilon\) and the
non-differentiated argument fixed,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial R(q,c)}{\partial q}
  =\frac{R(q,c)}{q}>0,
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial R(q,c)}{\partial c}
  =(1-\varepsilon)\frac{R(q,c)}{c}<0.
  \label{eq:p02-return-derivatives}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Boundary and accounting checks}
\label{subsec:p02-boundary-accounting}

The Phase~2 kernel satisfies the following checks:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item As \(q\downarrow0\), both \(\pi(q,c)\) and \(R(q,c)\) converge to
  zero.
  \item As \(c\to\infty\), both values converge to zero because
  \(1-\varepsilon<0\).
  \item If \(\varphi=0\), the commercial stream lasts one period in
  expectation and \(R(q,c)=\pi(q,c)\).
  \item As \(\varphi\uparrow1\),
  \(R(q,c)\to\pi(q,c)/(1-\beta)\), which remains finite because
  \(\beta<1\). The endpoint is a limiting check, not an admissible value
  under the frozen primitive domain \(\varphi\in[0,1)\).
  \item The restriction \(\varepsilon>1\) is necessary for a finite interior
  markup price under this residual-demand specification.
\end{enumerate}

The object \(R(q,c)\) is gross operating present value. The marginal cost
\(c\) is subtracted exactly once through the operating margin \(p-c\).
Route-specific fixed organizational costs, the institutional burden on route
\(E\), and any separately defined qualified-capacity payment are absent from
\(R\); later route-value equations must specify whether each manufacturing
cost is embedded in a route-specific marginal cost or subtracted separately,
and must not do both. All legacy reduced-form return symbols are inactive in
the rebuilt baseline; their deletion and replacement by the chain
\(y\rightarrow\pi\rightarrow R\) is recorded in the Phase~12 crosswalk.

Nothing in this section gives MAH a direct effect on \(A\), \(q\),
\(\varepsilon\), \(\beta\), or \(\varphi\). Thus Phase~2 establishes a
policy-invariant commercial value kernel, not a policy comparative static.
