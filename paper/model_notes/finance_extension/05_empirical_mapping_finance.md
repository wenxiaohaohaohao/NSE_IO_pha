# Secondary Empirical Mapping for Financing Capacity

Financing is a secondary empirical heterogeneity, not the baseline firm type.
Any proxy must be measured with validated pre-policy information.
Candidate interfaces include registered or paid-in capital, liquid assets,
leverage or debt capacity, listed status, VC/PE or parent backing, and prior
external-financing history. These are candidate proxies, not claims that a
specific data source is already usable.

Only after the baseline organizational effect and heterogeneity by internal
manufacturing capability are established should the analysis test

\[
MAH\times\text{weak internal manufacturing capability}
\times\text{limited/intermediate pre-policy financing capacity}.
\]

while controlling for size, age, ownership, business-group affiliation,
listed status, and pre-policy innovation history. The preferred outcomes are holder--manufacturer separation, development-stage
project advancement, and retained approvals or realized products. Patent
applications are not a baseline endogenous outcome. The baseline mechanism is
distinct from a research--development allocation model: MAH changes the
capital intensity of the retained commercialization organization but does not
increase financing supply. Promotion into the baseline requires an independent
post-control prediction for holder--manufacturer separation and new user
authorization.
