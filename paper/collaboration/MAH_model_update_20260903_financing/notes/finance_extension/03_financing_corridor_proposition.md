# Financing Corridor Proposition

For fixed \((q,m,k_i,p_m)\), suppose \(J_E(m)<J_I(m,k_i)\). Define

\[
W_i^0=\max\{\widetilde W_i^I,W^T,0\},\qquad
W_i^1=\max\{W_i^0,\widetilde W_i^E\}.
\]

Then \(W_i^1-W_i^0=[\widetilde W_i^E-W_i^0]_+\), and the strict-gain set is

\[
\mathcal C_i(p_m,\ell_i)=
\{(q,m):J_E(m)\le\ell_i,\ W_i^E(q,m;1,p_m)>W_i^0(q,m;\ell_i)\}.
\]

The financing regions are:

1. \(\ell_i<J_E\): entrusted production is not financeable; the reform has
   zero effect absent another mechanism.
2. \(J_E\le\ell_i<J_I\): the financing corridor; entrusted production is the
   only financeable retained route and yields a strict gain if it beats
   transfer and abandonment.
3. \(\ell_i\ge J_I\): internalization is again available, so the reform gain
   is the positive part of the entrusted value relative to all old options.

Within either policy regime, increasing \(\ell_i\) weakly expands the feasible
route set and hence weakly raises optimized value and advancement. The reform
treatment effect \(\Delta x_i(\ell_i)\), however, has no global monotone sign:
larger \(\ell_i\) can expand the pre-MAH internal option as well as the
post-MAH entrusted option. At \(J_E\) or \(J_I\), an ordinary derivative need
not exist.
